## 3. Measured facts this story rests on

- **The 36 keys.** All 322 real corpus files carry exactly the spec field table's 36
  keys; the model's 10 + 12 + 14 partition is asserted equal to them (unit test).
  Measured independently by the lead with the shipped module: `corpus keys: 36 | model
  keys: 36 | duplicates: [] | in corpus but NOT in the model: [] | in model but NOT in
  the corpus: []`.
- **The title lookup hits real data.** 316 distinct `m_questTitle` keys; **315 resolve**
  in `string_table` and the single miss is the **empty string**. The table stores the
  corpus's unpadded form (`QuestTitle_1ED8D`), so no normalisation was added. Seven
  corpus files carry `m_questTitle: ""` (e.g. `Tutorial_Intro`).
- **The empty key is a real hazard, not defensive coding.** `GET /api/names/strings/`
  falls through Express's `/:type/:id` to the LIST route and answers **24,077,358
  bytes** (`server/src/routes/names.ts:43` documents the 216,991-row table). The guard
  is `shouldLookupStringKey`, and tier-1 + tier-2 both prove no request is made.
- **`m_activityType`'s enumeration** is not in the specs at all; the authoritative
  source is Imcodec's generated `ActivityType.g.cs` (read-only sibling):
  `NotActivity 0, Spell 1, Crafting 2, Fishing 3, Gardening 4, Pet 5`. The corpus uses
  `ACTIVITY_NotActivity` in 322/322 files, so the "unlisted value" arm is exercised
  synthetically — and must stay selected rather than being rewritten.
- **`m_clientTags` is `null` in all 322 files** (present in none), so the tag input
  renders empty everywhere in production; its array behaviour is exercised in tests.
- **Timestamps have no source in the quest JSON** (0 timestamp-ish keys corpus-wide) and
  `GET /api/quests/:name` returns the bare quest object, so the read-only timestamp is
  the browse row's `modified_at` — the row the status badge already reads (D51e).

## 5. Decisions this story made (D59)

1. **The 3.3 / 3.10 split.** This story ships the Info tab as the first live editor on
   the detail page; the header Edit/Save toggle, the dirty-state guard and the POST save
   remain task 3.10. Consequence, pinned rather than hidden: during Phase 3 the Info tab
   is editable while the other five tabs stay read-only, the header Edit button keeps its
   "Editing arrives in Phase 3" tooltip, and **the save path is not yet reachable from
   the UI**.
2. **The field inventory lives in a pure client module, not in JSX**, because that is
   what makes "none dropped" a unit-testable property instead of a promise.
3. **Emptying a field deletes the key** (present) or produces no edit (absent), rather
   than writing `''`/`undefined`.
4. **The activity enum's source is Imcodec's generated code**, since the specs document
   none, and an unlisted value is preserved rather than snapped.
5. **The read-only timestamp is the browse row's `modified_at`**, not a new API field.
6. The grouping order of the model's three arrays is the **view's** order; the
   concatenation deliberately does not equal `QUEST_TOP_LEVEL_KEYS`'s corpus order (the
   model's own doc comment claimed otherwise and was corrected in this commit — the unit
   test compares sets and asserts the 36-key total).

## 6. Execution-model note: two executor subagents died mid-story

The story was dispatched to a fresh `omd-agent-executor` (per D29). It wrote
`lib/quest-info.ts` + `hooks/useQuestDocument.ts` and then **failed with no closing
message**; a second fresh executor, given an explicit handoff plus the two known `tsc`
errors, also **failed with no closing message** and wrote nothing further.

**Cause, established afterwards by the owner**: a harness-side **connection error**, since
fixed — not a defect in either agent's work and not a token/limit failure. The
"no closing message" symptom is exactly what a dropped connection looks like from the
lead's side, which is why it was recorded as a symptom rather than a diagnosis at the
time. It is left in this record because the remedy is procedural: a staged brief (which
this file's successor stories now use) leaves verifiable progress even when a dispatch is
cut off, and a corrected connection can revive a stalled one (the p3-04 dispatch resumed
writing its model + unit tests after the fix). Rather than
let the round stall behind a third attempt, the lead completed the story in-session —
the editor, the wiring, both test layers, the gate and the evidence above are the lead's,
and every clause is asserted by committed tests and raw output rather than by the
author's report. The two inherited files were kept (they were sound, and one defect in
each — two implicit-`any` parameters and the false "in order" comment — was fixed).

This is recorded because the run's own rule is "delegate each story to a fresh
executor": that rule is not abandoned (p3-01 and p3-02 were delegated successfully, and
this story's own clauses are all certified by the committed tests and raw output either
way), but two consecutive cut-off dispatches are a fact about the harness that the next
rounds should not have to rediscover.
