## What it independently established
- over ALL 322 real corpus quests: a freshly loaded document is never dirty; a round of edits
  (m_questTitle, and m_goals[0].m_goalTitle / m_level where they exist) makes it dirty; reset
  restores it BYTE-EXACTLY (documentsEqual AND JSON.stringify equality, so key order included);
  and the dirty flag is false again after the reset.
- dirty is an honest byte comparison, not an "an edit happened" flag: editing a value back to the
  loaded one is CLEAN again.
- a key-ORDER-only change (same values, different order) is DIRTY, and documentsEqual correctly
  reports byte-inequality - i.e. the document's bytes, not just its values, are the contract.
- markQuestDocumentSaved advances the baseline, so a saved document is clean and a later reset
  restores the SAVED document (not the originally loaded one).
- an untouched document resets to itself, and a second reset is a no-op.

## My own errors, recorded
Three of my assertions failed on first runs and every one was mine:
1. I guessed that editQuestDocument took ONE edit; it takes an ARRAY of edits.
2. I edited m_goals[0].m_goalTitle unconditionally, and the shared primitives refuse to invent an
   intermediate (a quest whose m_goals is absent cannot have m_goals[0] set) - my sweep now edits
   only paths that exist.
3. I expected documentsEqual to ignore key order; it is byte-level BY DESIGN, so a reorder is
   "not equal" there while a value-level comparison still says equal. I corrected my expectation
   rather than the model.
